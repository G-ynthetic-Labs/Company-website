Apps · CubeX · 8 × 8 × 8 · 512 cells

Chess was always meant to be flat. Now it isn't.

CubeX is a tactical strategy game played inside a cube of 512 cells, with an AI that perceives instead of searching and shows you its reasoning, term by term, every move. Raise an AI commander, decide what it should want, and watch it fight. Or take the board yourself.

Android · Coming to Google Play
There is no back rank

Not chess in a cube. A ruleset written for one.

Every piece has up to 26 neighbours instead of 8. Your chess instincts transfer. Your sense of safety does not.

The two-legged turn

A Rook that moves fewer than four cells doesn't end its turn. It turns 90° and keeps going. Move long to buy ground; move short to keep the turn alive.

The Fractal Knight

Leaps twice every turn. The second leap changes all three coordinates at once, the one move that can't be blocked or read from a flat view.

Pawns with no rear

Pawns move one cell along any of six directions and may never take back their own last move.

No checkmate

You win by capturing the King. Check is a warning the game won't enforce for you, so a brave sacrifice is a legal move.

The AI

It doesn't search. It perceives.

No game tree and no lookahead. The AI reads every one of the 512 cells, scores each legal move once, and takes the best. Because there is one scored list per turn, the explanation is the computation: open the reasoning panel to see every move it considered, every score broken into its terms, and the best capture it declined.

Then change it. Seven dials (material, mobility, morale, opportunism, caution, positional sense, chaos) change how it plays on its very next move.

THREE ADVANTAGES

Risk, Reward, Relation

Material, board control and the health of your command structure, kept as three separate margins. Kill a Commander and you weaken the General above it and orphan the pawns below it. Structure is a target.

The basics
